# 3D Animation Short

Read `../references/h3-production-contract.md` before authoring.

Create stylized 3D shorts as a sequence of controllable H3 scenes with stable design and explicit continuity. Do not compress a long narrative into one incoherent generation.

## Intake and scope

Use the refinement sheet to resolve premise, protagonist, objective, obstacle, emotional turn, ending, visual medium, audience, aspect ratio, total duration, and audio/dialogue. Ask whether the user wants one scene, a storyboard/plan, or a fully assembled short. If the idea exceeds 15 seconds, divide it into scenes before generation.

## Production bible

Create locks for each recurring character: silhouette, face, proportions, age/species, hair, costume construction, materials, accessories, palette, scale, movement character, and forbidden drift. Create environment locks for architecture, layout, landmark positions, material language, light direction, weather, and scale. Create a prop-state list for objects that change.

Use ordered cast references only for identity and design. Use separate visual references for environment/style. Never merge character traits across references. When a design is not approved, use the local formatter to turn the user’s idea into two or three concise design directions and ask for a choice before expensive scene generation.

## Story and scene gate

Express the story as setup → pressure/action → turn → payoff. Every scene must have one purpose, an incoming state, an observable action, an outgoing state, and an intentional last frame for chaining. Present the scene list and key continuity facts before queueing a multi-scene production.

## Performance and camera

Within each 24 fps H3 scene, stage anticipation, commitment, peak action, reaction, and settle. Describe weight, balance, contact, eye focus, hand/foot placement, cloth/hair response, and environmental reaction when relevant. Preserve screen direction, geography, eyelines, scale, prop state, costume, and lighting across cuts.

Choose lenses and camera moves that reveal performance. Avoid camera motion that competes with key animation, impossible repositioning, repeated actions, or generic “epic” coverage. Use the prior scene’s last frame as the next opening reference whenever a continuous handoff is required.

The local formatter may enrich staging, performance, camera, atmosphere, transitions, and audio, but cannot alter the approved story, locks, dialogue, or continuity states. OpenMagia validates every scene’s references and timing.

## Assembly and QA

Generate/review scene by scene. Check identity, silhouette, scale, costume, prop state, geography, screen direction, lighting, physical contact, dialogue length, and outgoing frame before continuing. Assemble in planned order with native effects/ambience and a coherent score policy.

If identity drifts, strengthen ordered references and simplify framing; if action is unclear, add anticipation/contact/reaction; if continuity breaks, regenerate from the approved last frame; if pacing is rushed, split the scene rather than adding more cuts.
