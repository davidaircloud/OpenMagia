# Kinetic Type Conductor

Read `../references/h3-production-contract.md` before authoring.

Create a short performance clip in which one designated referenced character appears to physically conduct two exact text phrases. Supporting Cast may appear, but only the designated conductor controls the typography. Preserve every attached character’s identity while flat typography responds causally to the conductor’s body movement and camera energy.

## Required inputs

Use the refinement sheet to collect:

- `TEXT A`: the first exact visible phrase;
- `TEXT B`: the second exact visible phrase;
- one Cast character or ordered identity references;
- optional setting, wardrobe lock, palette, music character, and aspect ratio override.

Default to `TEXT A = "MOVE WITH INTENT"` and `TEXT B = "FRAME THE FUTURE"` only when the user provides no copy. Preserve spelling, capitalization, punctuation, and language verbatim. The video must contain no other visible words, letters, numerals, logos, captions, or interface elements.

If either phrase is too long to remain readable during motion, ask the user to shorten it instead of silently rewriting it. Treat each referenced character’s face, hair or surface, anatomy, proportions, wardrobe silhouette, materials, accessories, and colors as identity locks. Name one conductor explicitly. Supporting characters perform simple reactions or actions and never conduct, duplicate, or obscure the approved typography. Do not introduce unreferenced performers.

## Direction system

The causal order is always:

1. the performer initiates a clear physical action;
2. the camera reacts to that action;
3. the typography follows the same force, direction, and stopping point.

Typography never moves randomly or leads the performer. A hand sweep sends a phrase laterally; a pull gathers separated letters; a turn bends a phrase around an arc; a push accelerates it toward the lens; a complete body stop freezes it. Keep the performer as the visual owner even during the fastest graphic moments.

Use sharp, flat 2D type without extrusion, bevels, inflated letters, or persistent internal deformation. Default to white with electric cyan and acid-yellow accent states. Change color as a brief beat response, never as a gradient. Tracking expansion, staggered follow-through, fragmentation, and reassembly may appear for only a few frames before the phrase returns to a fully readable state.

Create depth through placement and camera parallax rather than 3D letterforms. Place phrases near the lens, between camera and performer, or behind the performer. Maintain credible occlusion as type passes behind the body or crosses in front of a limb. Near type travels larger and faster; distant type travels smaller and slower.

## Signature 15-second choreography

Adapt framing and gesture to the character, but preserve the following dramatic progression and exact continuous timing:

- `CUT 01 | 0.00–1.20s` — Begin wide with the performer centered but already poised to move. `TEXT A` spans the near foreground while a smaller `TEXT B` sits deep behind the performer. On the opening impact, the camera dives through a gap in `TEXT A` and settles into a medium-wide view. One short segment of `TEXT A` flashes cyan after the pass.
- `CUT 02 | 1.20–2.55s` — The performer steps diagonally and draws one hand inward. Separate letters of `TEXT B` arrive from the edges along the hand’s pull, lock into the exact phrase beside the torso, and stop when the hand stops. The camera counterslides without covering the face.
- `CUT 03 | 2.55–4.00s` — From a low three-quarter angle, the performer sweeps an arm upward across the body. `TEXT A` rises from behind the legs, crosses into the foreground, briefly expands its tracking, and snaps back to readable spacing as the camera cranes upward.
- `CUT 04 | 4.00–5.80s` — Time suspension. Freeze the performer, hair or surface, clothing, accessories, `TEXT A`, and `TEXT B` completely. Only the camera travels in a fast 140-degree arc from the performer’s side toward the opposite front quarter. Hold `TEXT A` near-camera and `TEXT B` behind the performer so their occlusion and parallax change naturally while neither phrase animates.
- `CUT 05 | 5.80–7.15s` — Time restarts on a strong beat. The performer completes the interrupted motion and thrusts one palm toward camera. `TEXT A` accelerates past the palm toward the lens, fills the frame without becoming 3D, and creates a hard graphic transition.
- `CUT 06 | 7.15–8.75s` — Reveal a wide lateral composition. The performer advances across frame while the camera tracks in the opposite direction. `TEXT B` travels as a stable readable layer behind the performer; small spacing pulses follow footfalls without changing any letter.
- `CUT 07 | 8.75–10.45s` — Snap to a close medium view. The performer rotates a wrist, then closes the hand. `TEXT A` follows a shallow circular path around the shoulder, separates for a few frames, and resolves instantly into the exact phrase beside the face. Keep both eyes and defining facial features unobstructed.
- `CUT 08 | 10.45–12.90s` — Build a rhythmic escalation through three concise hard cuts: an extreme low angle, a side tracking pass, and a close view of the controlling hand. Vary performer scale and camera axis in every cut. Alternate `TEXT A` and `TEXT B` across foreground and background with brief cyan or yellow accent changes, but always restore complete readable phrases before each cut ends.
- `CUT 09 | 12.90–15.00s` — Finish with one continuous hero move. Orbit from the rear quarter to the front as the performer opens both hands. `TEXT A` enters from near-camera and `TEXT B` emerges from behind the performer; both settle on different depth planes framing the body. Expand tracking immediately before the final beat, then return to exact spacing. Freeze performer and typography together in a stable, readable hero composition for the final 0.45 seconds.

## Camera and audio

Combine motivated dolly movement, lateral passes, low angles, short orbits, whip transitions, and snap reframing. Contrast rapid movement with absolute stillness, but do not repeat the same composition. Avoid motion blur whenever it harms identity or text readability.

Use a fast, angular instrumental without vocals or dialogue. Synchronize body impacts, camera acceleration, typography travel, cuts, freeze, and restart to the beat. Pull the mix down immediately before time suspension, retain only a spatial tail while frozen, and restore the full beat when motion resumes.

## Refinement boundary and validation

The local formatter may adapt gestures to the referenced anatomy, improve camera paths, clarify occlusion, and describe sound accents. It must not alter `TEXT A`, `TEXT B`, character identity, reference roles, duration, beat ranges, typography dimensionality, or the rule that no other text appears.

Before generation verify: exactly one designated conductor; no more than two supporting Cast members in the same clip; exactly two approved phrases; the active conditioning mode matches the images actually sent; duration and every CUT boundary agree; no timing gaps or overlaps; physically motivated type motion; flat readable typography; credible foreground/background occlusion; complete stillness during time suspension; and a stable final hero hold.

If generated letters mutate, reduce the amount of simultaneous letter animation and strengthen the fully readable holds. If identity drifts, reduce cut density and reinforce ordered character references. If the result feels chaotic, preserve the gestures and remove secondary graphic accents before simplifying the performer or camera.
