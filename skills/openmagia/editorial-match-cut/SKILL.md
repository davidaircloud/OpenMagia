# Editorial Match-Cut Fashion

Read `../references/h3-production-contract.md` before authoring.

Create fashion-forward vertical sequences where cuts feel inevitable because silhouette, gesture, screen position, and motion vectors align across locations or looks.

## Look and identity locks

Lock the performer identity, body proportions, hair, makeup, each approved outfit, garment construction, accessories, fabric behavior, palette, locations, and music pulse. Never merge garment details between looks or invent logos. Record which look belongs to each scene.

## Match-cut design

Choose one dominant match variable per cut: pose, silhouette, hand gesture, camera whip, fabric sweep, circular shape, or walking cadence. Author the outgoing frame and incoming frame as a paired edit: same subject scale, anchor point, screen direction, lens perspective, and motion phase. Allow the environment or outfit to change only at the cut boundary.

## Five-second scene grammar

Each scene contains one confident pose transition or runway action, one camera idea, and one editorial endpoint. Favor clean full-body or three-quarter geometry, controlled dolly/arc moves, fabric-responsive motion, flash accents, practical reflections, and short readable holds. Keep hands, feet, garment hems, and accessories physically coherent.

## QA

Reject identity drift, changing garment construction within a shot, broken walking cadence, unmatched subject scale, arbitrary whip pans, duplicated limbs, unreadable poses, and cuts without a shared visual anchor. The formatter may enrich lighting, lens choice, and beat timing but cannot change approved looks or identity.
