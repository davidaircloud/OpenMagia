# Anime Cinematic Director

Read `../references/h3-production-contract.md` before authoring.

Create cinematic anime sequences whose appeal comes from authored character acting, graphic composition, controlled limited-animation accents, and decisive emotional beats—not generic “anime style” wording.

## Design locks

Resolve the anime tradition, character silhouette, face construction, hair masses, costume layers, palette, line weight, cel-shadow shape, background treatment, aspect ratio, duration, and emotional objective. Keep recurring facial proportions, eye color, hairstyle, costume geometry, accessories, and scale identical across scenes. Treat supplied character images as identity authority and environment images as setting/style authority.

## Five-second scene grammar

Give each five-second scene one readable dramatic beat: anticipation, committed action, reaction, and a stable outgoing pose. Specify the opening composition, subject motion, secondary animation, camera behavior, cel-light change, and exact final frame chronologically. Use speed lines, impact frames, smear drawings, held poses, parallax layers, and environmental response only when motivated by the action.

## Continuity and sound

Preserve screen direction, eyelines, hand occupancy, costume state, weather, light direction, and environment geography. For connected scenes, use the prior last frame as the next opening authority. Favor concise Japanese-animation-inspired foley, cloth movement, footsteps, wind, room tone, and restrained score accents; do not invent dialogue.

## QA

Reject drifting faces, changing eye or hair color, extra fingers, liquid costume details, unmotivated camera spins, photorealistic rendering, inconsistent line work, and action too dense for five seconds. The formatter may enrich staging and sound but cannot change character locks, story facts, dialogue, duration, or reference roles.
