# Hand-drawn Live Video

Read `../references/h3-production-contract.md` before authoring.

## Required intake

Establish the live location, real subject, drawn entity or mark, first contact point, intended interaction or transformation, ending state, duration, aspect ratio, tone, and reference roles. Ask only for facts that affect the scene. If the user supplied an image, identify whether it locks the environment, subject identity, drawing language, or opening frame.

Do not generate until the physical premise and ending are clear. If either is missing, the local refinement model must ask a short concrete question. When several treatments are viable, it should propose two or three compact directions such as playful pursuit, helpful transformation, or expressive environmental response, then let the user choose.

## Creative locks

Record the live subject identity and wardrobe; environment geometry and important surfaces; drawn entity shape, line language, palette, and personality; allowed contact and transformation; prohibited tonal escalation or additions; and exact ending image.

The formatter may improve clarity and motion, but cannot replace the requested entity, change identity, introduce horror, or turn the effect into polished CG.

## Interaction design

Build one continuous causal chain: discovery, first contact, physical response, morph or pursuit, peak interaction, and settle. Every transfer must have a visible route. Lines may crawl over a wall, wrap an object, jump only across a shown contact, or spill light onto nearby surfaces; they must not teleport.

Describe contact evidence: occlusion, contact shadow, reflected light, surface deformation, displaced dust, or a hand reacting to pressure. Retain irregular line weight, frame-to-frame boil, incomplete fills, registration wobble, and hand-made texture. Avoid uniform vector strokes and frictionless motion.

## H3 scene construction

Map the action chronologically within the requested duration. Specify the opening composition, camera behavior, subject action, drawn action, contact mechanics, environment response, and final hold. Handheld reaction should lag the event naturally while maintaining geography and screen direction.

Use a first-frame image only when the opening must match exactly. Use broader visual references for subject, environment, or drawing style and name each reference role explicitly. Do not ask H3 to reproduce off-screen geography that the references do not establish.

## Local refinement

The local model converts the approved treatment and user answers into the complete H3 prompt. It may strengthen verbs, timing, camera delay, material response, negative constraints, and sound cues. It must preserve all creative locks, surface uncertainties instead of guessing, and return a reviewable scene before generation.

## Audio and quality check

Synchronize room tone, pencil or charcoal scratch, electrical fizz if motivated, surface taps, fabric movement, and reaction sounds. Verify that identity holds, the contact path is visible, camera motion remains legible, the effect stays hand-drawn, and the final image resolves the premise.

If the result floats or teleports, simplify the action and add an explicit contact route. If it looks like CG, increase line boil and material imperfections. If geography drifts, reduce camera travel and anchor the interaction to one surface.
