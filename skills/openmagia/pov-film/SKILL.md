# POV Short Film

Read `../references/h3-production-contract.md` before authoring.

Create strict first-person scenes in which the camera is the viewer’s eyes and every movement is reachable from the established body and space.

This is a hard camera invariant, not a stylistic preference. Never describe the viewer-character in third person as visible in the shot. Never write “camera follows,” “camera pulls back to show/frame the character,” selfie coverage, over-the-shoulder coverage, reverse coverage, or an external view of the viewer-character. Refer to visible anatomy only as “the camera wearer’s left/right hand,” forearms, knees, feet, or body-edge cues entering from anatomically plausible screen positions. Other people may be visible because the viewer looks at them; they must never become the camera wearer or cause the camera to leave the wearer’s eyes.

## Intake and POV lock

Use the refinement sheet to resolve viewer identity, visible hands/body/clothing, starting pose, objective, location, route, obstacles, interaction, emotional tone, duration, aspect ratio, and final gaze target. Ask whether cuts are allowed; default to one continuous shot.

Build a POV lock: eye height, handedness, visible sleeves/gloves, body scale, locomotion, camera stabilization, field of view, and forbidden external views. Build a geography lock with starting orientation, landmark positions, reachable surfaces, doors/openings, elevation, and destination.

If a first-frame reference is supplied, treat its body cues, weather, lighting, camera axis, and geography as binding at time zero. Do not reinterpret it as loose inspiration.

## Physical action plan

Have the local formatter expand the user’s objective into a causal sequence of gaze, head turn, step/balance, reach, contact, force, recoil, response, and settle. It may fill safe mechanics but must show them in the refinement preview. Never teleport, pass through solid objects, detach hands, reveal the viewer externally, or cut to third-person coverage.

At 24 fps, assign exact ranges to spatial phases. Preserve landmark order, parallax, screen direction, horizon behavior, and reachable distance. Camera shake follows footfalls, impacts, breath, or imbalance with motivated amplitude and slight delay; it is not constant decoration. Hands must enter from plausible body positions and make readable contact.

If cuts are necessary, motivate them subjectively—blink, occlusion, rapid turn—and restore an intelligible orientation. The local formatter cannot break POV integrity or geography. OpenMagia validates H3 duration and continuity.

## Sound and QA

Use viewer-centered sound perspective: breath/clothing close, contact at the hand, footsteps beneath, environment positioned by the current gaze/location. Music is optional and secondary.

Check for any external view, floating/doubled hands, impossible reach, spatial reset, horizon jump, wall penetration, unmotivated shake, repeated action, or unclear final gaze. Fix by simplifying the route/action or strengthening the opening reference, not by adding cuts.

Before returning a refined prompt, scan every beat for an external-camera verb. If any beat could be filmed by a camera looking at the viewer-character, rewrite it from the literal eye viewpoint. When a reference already establishes first-person hands, handlebars, tools, or held objects, preserve those cues as the opening camera authority unless the user explicitly requests a new setup.
