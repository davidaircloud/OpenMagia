# Papercraft Stop-Motion

Read `../references/h3-production-contract.md` before authoring.

## Required intake

Extract one learning goal or message, audience, factual constraints, desired takeaway, runtime, aspect ratio, material or palette preferences, exact labels, and supplied references. Determine whether references lock the object design, paper language, miniature set, palette, or first frame.

If the takeaway or factual content is unclear, the local refinement model must ask before proposing visuals. When the metaphor is not prescribed, it should offer two or three constructible treatments—such as layered reveal, folding transformation, or modular assembly—and state how each communicates the idea.

## Truth and material locks

List every fact, number, label, sequence, brand element, and prohibited claim. Separately define paper weight and fiber, printed or hand-marked finish, fold style, tabs and slots, cut-edge character, miniature scale, set layers, lighting, and palette.

The formatter cannot alter facts or exact labels. Objects must remain physically buildable from the stated pieces. Avoid rubbery sheets, liquid morphing, glossy plastic, invisible construction, impossible hinges, or parts appearing without a visible source.

## Build plan

Inventory the required paper pieces and their initial positions. Define crease lines, hinges, tabs, slots, stacked layers, movable joints, labels, and the completed model. Plan each transformation as a discrete stop-motion action with a visible cause: a flap lifts, a tab slides, a panel folds, a layer stacks, or a cutout advances incrementally.

Use contact shadows, slight registration shifts, edge fibers, finger-made imperfections, and consistent miniature lighting to preserve tactile scale. Keep labels facing the camera and allow time to read them.

## H3 scene construction

Map the piece inventory and assembly beats into a chronological 24 fps scene. State the opening tabletop composition, macro camera behavior, each discrete construction step, factual reveal order, transition mechanics, and final completed model. The ending must visually communicate the approved takeaway without relying on unexplained narration.

Use a first-frame reference when the tabletop arrangement is fixed. Use broader references for material, object, or palette locks and name those roles. Keep camera moves modest enough that folds and slots remain legible.

## Local refinement

The local model may improve the metaphor, construction verbs, beat density, macro cinematography, tactile sound, and negative constraints only after the concept is approved. It must preserve facts, exact copy, material rules, and ending. It should flag an overfull runtime and recommend removing a beat instead of compressing the whole build.

## Audio and quality check

Synchronize paper snaps, crease rubs, blade or edge scrapes when appropriate, tabs clicking into slots, tabletop taps, and restrained room tone or music. Verify every part has an origin, folds use believable hinges, material stays papery, facts and labels remain correct, assembly order is readable, and the final model receives a clean hold.

If the paper melts or morphs, replace the transition with discrete folds and cuts. If the lesson is unclear, remove decorative beats and emphasize the factual reveal. If labels distort, use a clean surface and add deterministic OpenMagia overlays.
